Crypto, without the 80% wipeout.

It holds the strongest coins while the market is healthy, and steps aside into gold or cash when Bitcoin turns. You are not trying to be a genius. You are trying to still be here in two years.

The system ranks a pool of major crypto assets by relative strength, sizes positions by inverse volatility so one violent alt can't dominate the book, and goes fully defensive when Bitcoin breaks its long-term trend. The rules are the product. The coin pool is disclosed, and fixed in the indicator.

The honest part

Vero means true. So here is the whole truth, including the parts that are not flattering.

One year did a lot of the work.

2021 alone produced about half of everything the backtest made. Any multi-year crypto number deserves the same question: which single year did it come from?

The coin list was chosen with hindsight, and that flatters the record.

This is the most important sentence on the page. The twelve coins were picked knowing which ones survived. Testing the same rules on a survivorship-free universe — coins as they actually ranked at the time, including the ones that later went to zero — returns materially less. Plan around the rules and the protection, not around the historical multiple. The live record below is the honest test, and it is running now.

It protects. It does not make you immune.

Its worst drop was 26–37% depending on the day its rebalancing cycle started — typically 31% — peak to trough, from January 2018 to September 2026, measured on a survivorship-free universe — one that keeps the coins which later went to zero. Bitcoin fell 81.5% over that same window. On the chart, which starts in 2017 on the hand-picked pool, the panel shows the worst drop up to the latest day, recomputed every day. When the core protective rule was run on the far more brutal 2011–2017 era, it still took roughly 70% while Bitcoin took 85%. Real protection, in a genuinely violent market. Not a shield.

Most of the time you will be underwater.

About 92% of days sit below the best balance the system has ever reached. The longest flat stretch was typically about 15 months — between 12 and 24 depending on the start day — and typically 1 in 10 one-year starting points ended below where they began (between 1 in 18 and 1 in 7). This is normal for a system like this, and it is exactly where most people quit.

One line in the sand. If a drop of nearly 40%, or two and a half years without a new high, would make you quit, this is not for you. I would rather say that now than lose you in month 14.

Corrections

How to run it

Add the indicator to a daily (1D) chart of CRYPTO:BTCUSD (TradingView's Bitcoin chart, called INDEX:BTCUSD until November 2026), the only chart it works on, and create one alert on it, set to "Any alert() function call" — its headline says EXIT or ENTRY.

  • Act on an EXIT alert the same day. This is the one that matters — being two days late on exits destroyed a large share of the system's risk-adjusted performance in testing, because the protection only works if you actually get out.
  • ENTRY alerts are relaxed. Being a day or two late costs essentially nothing.

Getting out on time is the edge. Getting in is not urgent. That asymmetry is the single most important thing to know about running this.

How to verify it yourself

  • The backtest — load the Vero Compass indicator on TradingView and read its historical calls and figures directly, computed on your own chart from your own data feed. No trust in me required. The indicator: Vero Compass Navigator on TradingView (invite-only; ask for access there).
  • The live record: the log above is the forward record. Each call is pushed to this public repository the day it is logged. Commit times are set by our own computer, so what dates an entry is the push itself: from that day on, anyone could see and copy it.

Backtest vs live: historical performance shown by the indicator is a backtest and is independently verifiable there; this log is the live forward record from its inception date. The pool is a disclosed input, not a claimed edge — a backtest over a hand-picked list of coins that survived is survivorship-biased and is never presented here as a performance headline. Nothing here is back-dated or edited; a wrong call stays in the record. Your own figures may differ slightly by exchange feed.
